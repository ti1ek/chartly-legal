Privacy Policy

App: Chartly for macOS
Last updated: 1 September 2026

Chartly has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics SDK and no tracking, and we do not sell your data. The App reads a region of your screen that you select, analyses it, and shows you the result. This policy explains exactly what leaves your Mac, why, who receives it, and how long it is kept.

1. Who we are

Chartly is developed and operated by Tilek Koszhanov, an individual developer. For the processing described here, we act as the data controller. Contact details are in Section 15.

2. What we process, and why

  • The screen region you select. When you take a capture, the image of the region you drew is processed on your Mac and a downscaled copy (long edge up to 1024 px, JPEG) is sent to our service for analysis. Purpose: to identify what is on the chart. Without it there is no product.
  • Geometry the App measured. Candle positions, the formations found, and the levels detected, all as coordinates within that image. No prices — the App deliberately does not read the price axis.
  • Basic image metadata. Whether the chart is light or dark, whether it uses candles or bars, roughly how many candles it contains, and how confident the reader is. Purpose: to help the analysis interpret the picture.
  • A subscription identifier. An anonymous app user ID generated by RevenueCat, sent so the service can confirm your subscription is active before doing paid work. It is not linked to your name or email.

We do not ask for, and the App does not collect, your name, email address, contacts, location, browsing history, or the contents of your screen outside the region you deliberately select.

3. What your capture may contain

The App reads the region you draw, as it appears. If that region includes an account balance, an order ticket, a position size, a broker name, a window title or anything else visible on your screen, that content is part of the image and is sent for analysis along with the chart.

Select the chart area only. The App cannot tell which parts of the picture you consider sensitive, and it does not attempt to redact anything.

4. Screen Recording permission

macOS requires your explicit Screen Recording permission before any app can read the screen. Chartly requests it the first time you take a capture, uses it only while you are taking one, and never captures your screen in the background, on a schedule, or without you starting a capture. You can withdraw the permission at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording; the App will stop working and nothing else will change.

5. How analysis works (data flow)

  1. You select a region. The image is captured on your Mac.
  2. Your Mac reads the candles out of it locally and finds candidate formations. This step never leaves the device.
  3. The downscaled image, the measured geometry and the metadata in Section 2 are sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS/TLS) to our processing service, hosted on Vercel.
  4. Our service checks your subscription with RevenueCat, then forwards the image and the geometry to a third-party AI provider — currently Google (Gemini) and, as a fallback, OpenAI.
  5. The provider returns a structured reading — which formation matters, which measured level it points to, and a few typed observations. It returns no free text about you and no personal data.
  6. Our service passes that reading back to your Mac, which draws the annotated picture locally.

Our service stores nothing. The image exists only in memory for the duration of the request; it is never written to disk, to a database or to any storage bucket, and responses are sent with Cache-Control: no-store. Our server logs record only technical metadata — which provider answered, how many candidates and levels were offered, which identifier was chosen, and how long it took. Logs contain no images and no chart content.

If the service is unreachable, the App falls back to a purely local analysis on your Mac and nothing is sent at all.

6. Who we share data with

  • Google (Gemini API) and OpenAI — process the image to produce the reading. They act as our processors under their API terms, which provide that API content is not used to train their models. They may retain content briefly for abuse monitoring in accordance with their own policies.
  • Vercel — hosts the processing service and handles the request in transit.
  • RevenueCat — manages subscription status, keyed to the anonymous app user ID. RevenueCat also receives standard Apple Search Ads attribution data where Apple provides it.
  • Apple — processes all payments and manages your subscription. We never see your payment details.

We do not sell, rent or trade personal data, and we do not share it for advertising or profiling. We may disclose information where we are legally required to do so, or to establish or defend legal claims.

7. What stays on your Mac

  • Annotated captures are written to the App's own cache folder inside its sandbox container, so you can find a previous result. They are yours; they are never uploaded, and macOS removes them when it clears caches. You can delete them at any time from the App's History item.
  • The clipboard. Each result is copied to your clipboard so you can paste it. What you then do with it is outside the App.
  • Preferences — your shortcut and similar settings, stored locally.

8. International transfers

Our processing service and the providers listed above operate infrastructure in the United States and other countries, so your capture may be processed outside your country of residence, including outside the EEA and the UK. Where required, these transfers rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses or an equivalent safeguard offered by the relevant provider.

9. Retention

  • Images and chart content: not retained by us at all. Held in memory for the seconds a request takes, then discarded.
  • Server logs: technical metadata only, retained by our hosting provider for a short operational period (typically up to 30 days) and used for debugging and abuse prevention.
  • Subscription records: kept by RevenueCat and Apple for as long as needed to manage your subscription and to meet their legal and accounting obligations.
  • Local files on your Mac: kept until you or macOS delete them.

10. Legal bases (EEA/UK users)

  • Performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) — processing your capture to give you the analysis you asked for, and verifying your subscription.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) — keeping the service secure and available, preventing abuse, and debugging failures, using technical metadata only.
  • Legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)) — where we must retain or disclose information by law.

If a capture you submit happens to contain personal data, you are responsible for having a lawful basis to process it; see Section 3.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to the processing of your personal data, to data portability, and to lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority. Because we hold no images, no accounts and no identifiers beyond an anonymous subscription ID, there is usually very little for us to act on — but write to us at the address below and we will respond within the time your law allows. To delete everything the App holds locally, delete the App.

12. Security

All traffic between your Mac, our service and the providers is encrypted in transit (TLS). API keys live only on the server and are never shipped inside the App. Because nothing is stored, there is no database of your charts to be breached. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

13. Children

Chartly is not directed to children and is not suitable for them. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has provided personal data through the App, contact us and we will delete whatever we hold.

14. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. The "Last updated" date shows the latest version, and material changes will be reflected in the App or on this page.
